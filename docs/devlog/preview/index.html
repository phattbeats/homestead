Homestead devlog (drafts)

  1. Homestead devlog, Part 1: The pitch. One container, two people, take turns.
    How a two-person chores-and-calendar app became a public single-container PWA in July 2026: take-turns chores, an iframe shell with an escape dot, one SQLite file, and the first round of bugs.
  2. Homestead devlog, Part 2: The front door: logins, invites, and SSO
    How Homestead went from one password per person to invite links that create real accounts, Authentik sign-in you can link later, and a break-glass path for the owner, plus the cookie and secret rules that keep the door shut.
  3. Homestead devlog, Part 3: The Porch: a feed, then rooms
    How a complaint about Instagram became a chronological wall, then the front door of the whole app, and how everything else turned into rooms you add one at a time.
  4. Homestead devlog, Part 4: Calendars and libraries: other people's data
    How Homestead stopped asking the household to type things twice: CalDAV and Microsoft 365 calendars merged into one month grid, credentials locked up with AES-256-GCM, and Plex and Kavita synced into an entity graph where Dune is one node.
  5. Homestead devlog, Part 5: Agents move in: Hearth, tokens, and the third-party app contract
    Personal access tokens with exactly your powers, an oil lamp named Hearth that can queue a movie but deliberately can't touch the lights, and the app contract whose dogfood proved our scopes weren't being checked.
  6. Homestead devlog, Part 6: The Gazette, the brand, and the Forum
    A morning paper that refuses to pad a quiet day, a front door that finally opens, and the October call to make the Porch look like a 2006 message board.
  7. Homestead devlog, Part 7: How a pile of agents built a house app (and the fresh start)
    The last part: issues as units of work, a Definition of Done that wants a screenshot, a test chain that quietly skipped tests, a Dockerfile that kept forgetting folders, and why the repo now has exactly one commit.
